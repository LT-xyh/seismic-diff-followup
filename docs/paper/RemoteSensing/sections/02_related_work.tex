\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Seismic Velocity Reconstruction and the Choice of Observations}

The choice of observations is central to the inverse problem posed by a learning-based velocity model. Classical seismic velocity analysis and depth imaging derive their constraints from travel-time behavior and the propagation of wavefields, while full-waveform inversion seeks a velocity field whose simulated measurements agree with observed data \citep{dix1955velocities,stolt1978migration,pratt1998gaussnewton,virieux2009overview}. Deep inverse models replace repeated optimization at inference with a learned mapping, although the relation between their input and output remains specific to the training protocol. InversionNet and the OpenFWI benchmark exemplify direct velocity reconstruction from seismic waveforms \citep{wu2020inversionnet,deng2022openfwi}; subsequent network designs explore alternative connections, representations, and reconstruction capacity \citep{najafi2025svinvnet}. UPFWI, by contrast, incorporates a wave-equation-based training process rather than relying solely on supervised velocity labels \citep{jin2022upfwi}. These source methods cannot be treated as identical inversion procedures when their observations and learning objectives differ.

A processed-constraint reconstruction task has a different information structure. An RMS-velocity field summarizes propagation kinematics, a migrated section places energy in an approximate image domain, interpreted horizons delineate selected interfaces, and well logs contribute sparse depth-domain values. Such observations arise from distinct processing or interpretation operations and may share errors as well as useful information \citep{hampson2001multiattribute}. Their combination is consequently not equivalent to enlarging a raw-seismic input tensor. The present work studies reconstruction after these products are available; the source architectures used in its comparison are adapted to that common multimodal setting. This distinction is particularly important for the UPFWI-derived comparison, whose network backbone is used here as a supervised model without its original unsupervised wave-equation training loop.

\subsection{Deterministic Estimation and Generative Transport}

Deterministic encoder--decoder models generally learn a point estimate of velocity from the supplied observations. Structured and latent mappings change the representation in which that estimate is formed, providing a range of capacities and inductive biases \citep{yang2019deeplearning,wu2020inversionnet,feng2024autolinear,gupta2025gfi}. Such estimators are attractive when the desired output is a single velocity field and the available constraints strongly determine its broad organization. A full-field regression loss, however, does not explicitly distinguish the large-scale component from the correction that remains after background estimation. The two components can have different geophysical interpretations even when they are optimized within the same network.

Generative methods address the prediction problem from the perspective of distributions or conditional sampling. Adversarial models learn a data-dependent generator, whereas diffusion and score-based approaches recover target samples through a sequence of denoising steps \citep{ho2020ddpm,song2021scorebased}. Latent diffusion shifts that operation to an encoded representation, reducing the state dimension while introducing a learned decoding stage \citep{rombach2022ldm}. Posterior-oriented diffusion methods additionally illustrate how a generative prior may be combined with inverse-problem information, although their conditioning assumptions differ from those of directly supervised velocity reconstruction \citep{chung2023diffusionposterior}. Flow Matching describes generation through a vector field trained to follow a specified probability path; a standard linear interpolation gives an especially direct relation between the sampled state and its target velocity \citep{lipman2023flowmatching,liu2023rectifiedflow}. The generator's transported variable can nevertheless be chosen independently of that mathematical construction. In BG-RFM, the transported state is the correction to a learned background, not the entire depth-velocity field.

This selection is related to residual prediction but has a specific inference-time consequence. A fixed analytical or oracle reference and an observation-dependent prediction are not interchangeable when the final estimate must be composed without access to the target. BG-RFM supervises its generative correction relative to the same background estimate available at inference, while also supplying that estimate as vector-field context. In full-field coordinates, the resulting path is equivalent to standard transport from a background-centered stochastic base; the difference from direct full-field generation lies in the reconstruction reference and its connection to deterministic supervision, not in a new family of Flow-Matching equations.

\subsection{Multimodal Representation and Reconstruction Roles}

Multimodal learning studies the alignment and fusion of heterogeneous inputs and the recovery of useful representations when some modalities are missing or incomplete \citep{baltrusaitis2019multimodal,havaei2016hemis}. Contrastive objectives promote agreement between corresponding views \citep{tian2020cmc}, while Symile extends cross-modal alignment to tuples containing multiple modalities \citep{saporta2024symile}. These techniques can make geophysical evidence more accessible to a downstream predictor, but an aligned representation does not by itself specify how the predictor should allocate responsibility between smooth velocity variation and interface-related corrections.

The distinction between representation and reconstruction motivates the role-aware design investigated here. Both learned condition interfaces retain information from the available modalities; the background-oriented interface supplies a deterministic estimator, while the structure-oriented interface participates in the generative correction along with an embedding of the predicted background. Training-only wavelet anchors and reliability priors encourage meaningful organization of the condition space, but are not supplied as target-derived inputs during inference. This arrangement connects heterogeneous observation fusion to the variable chosen for reconstruction, thereby separating the organization of geophysical evidence from the mathematical mechanism used to transport the remaining correction.
