\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Task Definition and Synthetic Observation Contract}

The objective is to recover a depth-domain velocity field from geophysical products that have already been generated through seismic processing or interpretation. The observed information comprises four complementary constraints:
\[
X=\{M,H,V_{\mathrm{rms}},W\},
\]
where $M$ is a post-stack migrated image, $H$ is an interpreted-horizon map, $V_{\mathrm{rms}}$ is RMS velocity, and $W$ is a sparse well-velocity map with an associated mask. The target is the depth-domain velocity model
\[
V\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times70\times70}.
\]
This setting differs from end-to-end inversion of raw shot gathers: the model receives time-domain kinematic and imaging products together with depth-domain structural and local-velocity information. RMS velocity and the migrated image are represented on a $1000\times70$ time--lateral grid, while horizons, wells, and the target use the $70\times70$ depth grid. Their different sampling domains make feature alignment part of the reconstruction problem, rather than a neutral preprocessing detail.

The controlled benchmark draws on FlatVel-A/B, CurveVel-A/B, FlatFault-A/B, and CurveFault-A/B in OpenFWI \citep{deng2022openfwi}. Each depth-velocity target is paired with offline-computed RMS velocity, post-stack migrated imagery, and horizon information, together with sparse well columns sampled under the declared protocol. The training pipeline reads these products rather than estimating them jointly with the velocity field. The following construction steps specify what information each observation contributes and delimit the synthetic setting in which the reconstruction results should be interpreted.

\paragraph{RMS velocity.}
For each lateral position, depth samples are mapped to two-way time through
\begin{equation}
t(z,x)=2\int_0^z \frac{1}{V(z',x)}\,\mathrm{d}z',
\end{equation}
and the RMS field is computed after interpolation to a uniform 1-ms time grid:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2(t,x)=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t v^2(\tau,x)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.
\end{equation}
The integration uses the known synthetic velocity model and therefore supplies a particularly informative columnwise kinematic constraint. It should be distinguished from a field-derived RMS estimate, for which travel-time picking, bandwidth, and other processing uncertainties affect the available information. The controlled construction is a defining feature of the benchmark rather than an inference step performed by BG-RFM.

\paragraph{Post-stack migrated image.}
OpenFWI shot records are processed by common-midpoint grouping, normal-moveout correction using the associated RMS field, stacking, and Kirchhoff post-stack time migration. The resulting image provides a dense structural observation on the time--lateral grid.

\paragraph{Horizons and wells.}
The benchmark horizon map is generated from vertical velocity-gradient magnitude using a fixed quantile threshold to form a binary structural map. Sparse wells retain zero to three selected lateral columns. For each selected column, the full depth trace is copied into the well-value tensor and the binary well mask is one along that column; values are zero outside selected columns. Validation and test wells are deterministic under well seed 1234.

\subsection{Role-Aware Multi-Source Conditioning}

A direct concatenation of these observations would leave their geophysical roles to be resolved entirely by a common downstream predictor. BG-RFM instead learns two condition interfaces corresponding to different reconstruction responsibilities. Importantly, the role division does not exclude a modality from either route: each available source can inform both background recovery and structural correction, with its contribution determined by the learned encoder and reliability weighting. For modality $m$, the encoding yields
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_m(x_m)=\left(Z_m^{\mathrm{bg}},Z_m^{\mathrm{str}},r_m\right),
\end{equation}
where $r_m$ denotes the learned role-specific reliability. In the evaluated loader, a present non-well modality has quality state $q_{i,m}=1$, whereas the well state indicates whether any sampled well column exists. Thus availability is $a_{i,m}=\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{mask}_{i,m}q_{i,m}>0]$ and does not depend on target error. Adaptive average pooling followed by a linear layer and sigmoid estimates the reliability assigned to each role. Multiplying that score by availability prevents missing inputs from contributing to the fused condition; weights are then normalized across available modalities with the denominator clamped at $10^{-6}$. The fused weights remain zero when all modalities are unavailable. The two role-specific interfaces are
\begin{equation}
C_k=\mathcal G_k\left(\sum_{m\in\Omega}w_m^k Z_m^k\right),
\qquad k\in\{\mathrm{bg},\mathrm{str}\},
\end{equation}
with $\Omega$ the observed modality set. In the condition-learning objective, the corresponding roles are denoted numerical and structural; downstream, the numerical role forms the background-oriented interface $C_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and the structural role forms $C_{\mathrm{str}}$. RMS and post-stack migrated inputs are compressed/aligned to $70\times70$ spatial feature maps with 32 channels, while horizon and well streams produce 16-channel aligned maps; fusion occurs only after alignment to the depth-grid representation. RMS uses temporal pooling and bilinear alignment, while PSTM uses anisotropic downsampling and projection. Both $C_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{str}}$ are 32-channel $70\times70$ conditions. Training-only subset-Symile, anchor-calibration, separation, and reliability terms are defined below. Their target-derived anchor tensors are excluded from inference, which uses only observations, masks, and the supplied availability/quality state.

\subsection{Deterministic Background Prediction}\label{sec:methods-background}

The background prediction is intended to capture a smoothly varying velocity reference before generative correction is applied. Rather than requiring the residual generator to recover the entire regional velocity trend, a separately supervised full-resolution U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} with 192 hidden channels maps the background-oriented condition to an estimate:
\begin{equation}
\hat B=f_{\mathrm{bg}}(C_{\mathrm{bg}}).
\end{equation}
The target for this branch is the low-pass component defined by the same linear operator used in subsequent diagnostics and residual constraints:
\begin{equation}
P_L(X)=\operatorname{AvgPool}_{5\times5}(X;\,\text{stride}=1,\text{padding}=2),
\end{equation}
The pooling implementation includes padded values in the average, and the complementary operation is $P_H=I-P_L$. Supervision combines an absolute-error term with a squared-error term under the active background objective,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}
=
\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_1
+0.5\,\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_2^2.
\label{eq:bg-loss-final}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Prediction-Relative Flow Matching}\label{sec:methods-residual}

A residual generator requires a reference field, but an oracle low-pass target is unavailable for an unseen example. BG-RFM therefore defines the correction relative to the predicted background that will also be used at inference:
\begin{equation}
R_{\hat B}=V-\hat B.
\label{eq:rs_pred_residual}
\end{equation}
During residual optimization, this background remains the forward reference while the stop-gradient operation prevents residual losses from modifying the background branch through the estimated field:
\begin{equation}
\bar B=\operatorname{sg}[\hat B].
\end{equation}
The correction is thus conditioned both on the structure-oriented representation and on an embedding of the background estimate to be corrected:
\begin{equation}
c=[C_{\mathrm{str}};\psi_{\mathrm{bg}}(\bar B)].
\end{equation}

Conditional Flow Matching is applied to this prediction-relative target using a standard linear interpolation. With continuous time $t\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$ and Gaussian base sample $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$, the interpolated residual and its target vector field are
\begin{equation}
R_t=(1-t)\xi+t(V-\bar B),
\qquad
u_t=(V-\bar B)-\xi ,
\end{equation}
and the vector-field term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
=
\mathbb E\left[
\|v_\theta(R_t,t,c)-u_t\|_2^2
\right].
\end{equation}
The evaluated residual network is a full-resolution FiLM-conditioned U-Net \citep{perez2018film} with 128 hidden channels and an identity codec on the $70\times70$ velocity/state grid. Training samples continuous time $t\in[0,1]$; inference integrates the learned vector field with 50 explicit Euler steps.

The translated residual path also admits a useful full-field interpretation. The stop-gradient operation changes only back-propagation, so $\bar B$ and $\hat B$ have identical forward values. On defining
\begin{equation}
Y_t=\hat B+R_t
\end{equation}
therefore gives
\begin{equation}
Y_t=(1-t)(\hat B+\xi)+tV.
\label{eq:background-centered-path}
\end{equation}
The transformed path is the familiar linear transport from a stochastic base centered on the predicted background to the target field $V$. No new family of Flow-Matching equations is introduced; the substantive modeling decision is to learn an observation-dependent reference and retain it in both conditioning and final reconstruction.

Vector-field regression alone does not express the composed reconstruction error in the same form as the final velocity estimate. The active objective therefore supplements Flow Matching with an endpoint reconstruction term and a low-pass residual constraint. The endpoint estimate extrapolates from the sampled state at time $t$ using the learned vector field:
\begin{align}
\widetilde R
&=R_t+(1-t)v_\theta(R_t,t,c),\\
\mathcal L_R
&=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
+\|\bar B+\widetilde R-V\|_1
+0.2\,\|P_L(\widetilde R)-P_L(V-\bar B)\|_1.
\label{eq:residual-loss-final}
\end{align}
The residual branch does not back-propagate through $\bar B$; background parameters receive their direct background supervision instead.

\subsection{Prediction Consistency and Joint Objective}

The final velocity field is obtained by adding the transported correction to the same background prediction used to define and condition the residual task:
\begin{equation}
\hat V=\hat B+\hat R.
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:rs_pred_residual},
\begin{equation}
\hat V-V=\hat R-R_{\hat B}.
\label{eq:rs_composition_identity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} is an \emph{algebraic identity} that makes the error accounting induced by the shared background reference explicit.

Joint optimization uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_R+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}+0.01\,\mathcal L_C ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_C$ is the role-aware condition objective:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_C={}&
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{subsym}}^{\mathrm{str}}
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{subsym}}^{\mathrm{num}}
+0.5\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{anchor}}\\
&+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rel}}
+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{str,num}}^{\perp}
+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{unique,role}}^{\perp}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:condition-objective}
\end{equation}
The condition objective is designed to align heterogeneous evidence while retaining distinct interfaces for numerical background information and structural guidance. Its training-only references come from a one-level Haar decomposition of the target velocity: the resized LL band provides the numerical anchor and the L2 fusion of resized LH, HL, and HH bands provides the structural anchor, using replicate boundaries and nearest-neighbor resizing. These anchors are detached from the condition-loss computation and are not supplied at inference.

Subset-Symile is evaluated within groups of records sharing the same modality-availability pattern. Aligned same-record modality projections and their anchor form the positive tuple, while within-group batch derangements supply up to batch-size-minus-one negatives; groups with fewer than two records are skipped. Symmetric InfoNCE calibrates normalized modality and anchor projections, with a cosine-distance fallback for singleton anchor-calibration groups. The remaining active terms regularize the learned role reliabilities toward fixed modality-role priors and penalize absolute cosine similarity between pooled structural/numerical maps and between pooled unique/role representations. Appendix~\ref{app:math} specifies the reliability priors and the gradient routing used in this evaluated configuration.

Training is staged so that the condition representations and deterministic background estimator are established before residual transport is optimized. Condition pretraining runs for 100 epochs with batch size 150 and AdamW learning rate $10^{-4}$; the checkpoint is selected by validation retrieval top-5. The background stage then runs for 100 epochs with batch size 100 and learning rate $10^{-4}$, initialized from that condition checkpoint and selected by validation loss. Residual pretraining follows for 100 epochs with batch size 64 and learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$, starting from the background checkpoint and retaining a frozen identity codec; its checkpoint is also selected by validation loss.

Final joint optimization warm-starts from the selected condition, background, and residual checkpoints. It runs for 100 epochs with batch size 64 per process on four devices using distributed data parallelism and bfloat16 mixed precision. AdamW uses grouped learning rates of $10^{-6}$ for the encoder backbone, $3\times10^{-6}$ for encoder heads, contrastive parameters, and condition adapters, $10^{-4}$ for the background estimator, and $5\times10^{-5}$ for the residual vector field; the configured schedule has no learning-rate scheduler and retains the frozen codec. The validation-loss-selected joint checkpoint supplies the evaluated model. Its inference network contains 10,630,216 trainable parameters (10.63M); the encompassing training wrapper contains 10,648,427 parameters including training-only modules.

\subsection{Inference and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:methods-evaluation}

Inference begins with the available geophysical constraints and their mask/availability state; neither the target-derived Haar anchors nor any other supervised reference is required. BG-RFM first predicts $\hat B$ and then integrates one sampled residual trajectory through 50 explicit Euler steps before forming $\hat V=\hat B+\hat R$. The common evaluation uses batch size 64 and an unshuffled test loader (\texttt{shuffle=False}). To make the stochastic component reproducible, the BG-RFM base seed is fixed at 2027 and combined with the ordered identities in each evaluation batch through a deterministic hash. This batch-keyed construction determines the draw, not the held-out membership. Only one trajectory is evaluated per record, without ensemble averaging; the deterministic references produce direct predictions without a sampling seed.

The eight-subset corpus contains 336,000 aligned records, partitioned by a deterministic global 0.7/0.2/0.1 split with seed 42. This produces 33,600 held-out test records. The condition, background, residual, and joint stages use the same splitting implementation, and their loaders access the appropriate training or validation partition. The reconstructed evaluation manifest was checked for train/validation, train/test, and validation/test overlap, with none found; test scores do not participate in checkpoint selection. This common record contract is also used when evaluating the adapted reference models.

All reported velocity-error metrics use the normalized target coordinate, mapping the physical interval 1500--4500~m/s to $[-1,1]$. At data loading, continuous quantities are clamped to the declared profile range; the horizon and well-mask channels remain discrete. Predictions are evaluated without additional post-hoc clipping, so the scores describe the outputs actually produced by the retained checkpoints.

Metrics are computed per sample and then averaged over the 33,600 records. If $e_i=\hat V_i-V_i$ and $d=HW$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE}_i=\frac{\|e_i\|_1}{d},
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}_i=\sqrt{\frac{\|e_i\|_2^2}{d}}.
\end{equation}
The reported RMSE is the mean of per-sample RMSE values:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sqrt{\frac{1}{HW}\|\hat V_i-V_i\|_2^2}.
\label{eq:eval-rmse}
\end{equation}
SSIM is computed globally for each sample as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SSIM}_i=
\frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y+C_{1,i})(2\sigma_{xy}+C_{2,i})}
{(\mu_x^2+\mu_y^2+C_{1,i})(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+C_{2,i})}.
\label{eq:eval-ssim}
\end{equation}
SSIM is computed globally over each flattened $70\times70$ image using population variance/covariance, $C_{1,i}=(0.01L_i)^2$, $C_{2,i}=(0.03L_i)^2$, and
$L_i=\max(\max V_i-\min V_i,1)$; the resulting samplewise SSIM values are then averaged.

All matched-input reference models receive five channels representing the migrated image, RMS velocity, horizon map, masked well values, and the binary well mask. Their evaluation checkpoints follow their respective documented training protocols: InversionNet, VelocityGAN, and the UPFWI-derived supervised backbone use the final checkpoints of fixed 100-epoch schedules, while the Latent U-Net adaptation uses its minimum-validation-loss checkpoint. BG-RFM is evaluated with the selected final-joint-stage checkpoint. These selection rules are fixed independently of the test metrics; Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} documents the source-backbone adaptations and parameter-count conventions.
