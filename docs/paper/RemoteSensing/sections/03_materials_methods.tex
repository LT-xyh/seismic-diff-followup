\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Task Definition and Synthetic Observation Contract}

This study considers velocity-model reconstruction when four processed or interpreted constraints are already available:
\[
X=\{M,H,V_{\mathrm{rms}},W\},
\]
where $M$ is a post-stack migrated image, $H$ is an interpreted-horizon map, $V_{\mathrm{rms}}$ is RMS velocity, and $W$ is a sparse well-velocity map with an associated mask. The target is the depth-domain velocity model
\[
V\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times70\times70}.
\]
The task is therefore not raw-shot end-to-end inversion. RMS velocity and the migrated image are represented on a $1000\times70$ time--lateral grid, while horizons, wells, and the target use the $70\times70$ depth grid.

The benchmark is constructed from FlatVel-A/B, CurveVel-A/B, FlatFault-A/B, and CurveFault-A/B in OpenFWI \citep{deng2022openfwi}. The processed/interpreted constraints are generated offline from each synthetic reference model and its associated seismic data. This constitutes a \emph{controlled synthetic observation contract}. It differs from a field setting in which RMS velocity, horizons, migration images, and wells have heterogeneous and uncertain estimation errors.

\paragraph{RMS velocity.}
For each lateral position, depth samples are mapped to two-way time through
\begin{equation}
t(z,x)=2\int_0^z \frac{1}{V(z',x)}\,\mathrm{d}z',
\end{equation}
and the RMS field is computed after interpolation to a uniform time grid:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2(t,x)=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t v^2(\tau,x)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.
\end{equation}
Because this quantity is derived directly from the known synthetic target, it can encode substantially more columnwise velocity information than an RMS product estimated from noisy, band-limited field observations. The benchmark therefore measures reconstruction under informative processed constraints, not performance under realistic RMS-estimation uncertainty.

\paragraph{Post-stack migrated image.}
OpenFWI shot records are processed by common-midpoint grouping, normal-moveout correction using the associated RMS field, stacking, and post-stack time migration. The resulting image provides a dense structural observation on the time--lateral grid.

\paragraph{Horizons and wells.}
The benchmark horizon map is generated from strong vertical velocity gradients using a fixed quantile rule. Sparse wells retain zero to three selected velocity columns together with a binary mask. Validation and test wells are deterministic under well seed 1234.

\subsection{Role-Aware Multi-Source Conditioning}

The modalities differ in sampling domain, density, and information content, but BG-RFM does not impose a one-modality/one-role partition. Each modality is encoded into background-oriented and structure-oriented representations,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_m(x_m)=\left(Z_m^{\mathrm{bg}},Z_m^{\mathrm{str}},r_m\right),
\end{equation}
where $r_m$ contains learned role-specific reliability scores. Availability, externally supplied quality, and predicted reliability define normalized fusion weights. The two role interfaces are
\begin{equation}
C_k=\mathcal G_k\left(\sum_{m\in\Omega}w_m^k Z_m^k\right),
\qquad k\in\{\mathrm{bg},\mathrm{str}\},
\end{equation}
with $\Omega$ the observed modality set. The final model uses 16-channel modality/role features and a 32-channel structural condition adapter. Training-only alignment, anchor-calibration, separation, and reliability terms are specified in Supplementary Section~S1; they are not inference inputs.

\subsection{Deterministic Background Prediction}

The background branch is a full-resolution U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} with 192 hidden channels:
\begin{equation}
\hat B=f_{\mathrm{bg}}(C_{\mathrm{bg}}).
\end{equation}
The supervision target uses the exact low-pass operator implemented throughout the study,
\begin{equation}
P_L(X)=\operatorname{AvgPool}_{5\times5}(X;\,\text{stride}=1,\text{padding}=2),
\end{equation}
with the framework's default inclusion of padded values in the average. The complementary operator is $P_H=I-P_L$. The active background objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}
=
\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_1
+0.5\,\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_2^2.
\label{eq:bg-loss-final}
\end{equation}
No background high-frequency penalty, multiscale term, or learned quality gate is active in the evaluated configuration.

\subsection{Prediction-Relative Flow Matching}

The residual target is defined relative to the background that is actually available at inference:
\begin{equation}
R_{\hat B}=V-\hat B.
\label{eq:rs_pred_residual}
\end{equation}
During residual optimization, the forward value of the prediction is preserved but detached from the residual gradient path,
\begin{equation}
\bar B=\operatorname{sg}[\hat B].
\end{equation}
The residual vector field receives the structural condition and a learned embedding of this detached background,
\begin{equation}
c=[C_{\mathrm{str}};\psi_{\mathrm{bg}}(\bar B)].
\end{equation}

BG-RFM uses the standard linear Flow-Matching path. For $t\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$ and $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$,
\begin{equation}
R_t=(1-t)\xi+t(V-\bar B),
\qquad
u_t=(V-\bar B)-\xi ,
\end{equation}
and the vector-field term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
=
\mathbb E\left[
\|v_\theta(R_t,t,c)-u_t\|_2^2
\right].
\end{equation}
The evaluated residual network is a full-resolution FiLM-conditioned U-Net \citep{perez2018film} with 128 hidden channels and an identity codec on the $70\times70$ velocity grid.

The prediction-relative formulation also has a simple full-field interpretation. Because the stop-gradient operator changes only the backward path, $\bar B$ and $\hat B$ have the same forward value. Defining
\begin{equation}
Y_t=\hat B+R_t
\end{equation}
therefore gives
\begin{equation}
Y_t=(1-t)(\hat B+\xi)+tV.
\label{eq:background-centered-path}
\end{equation}
In full-field coordinates, prediction-relative residual Flow Matching can thus be viewed as standard linear transport from a stochastic base centered on the predicted background toward $V$. BG-RFM does not introduce a new mathematical class of Flow Matching; its contribution is the geophysical reconstruction organization and the shared predicted-background reference used across supervision, conditioning, and composition.

The residual loss also includes the composed reconstruction and the low-pass content of the actual prediction-relative residual:
\begin{align}
\widetilde R
&=R_t+(1-t)v_\theta(R_t,t,c),\\
\mathcal L_R
&=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
+\|\bar B+\widetilde R-V\|_1
+0.2\,\|P_L(\widetilde R)-P_L(V-\bar B)\|_1.
\label{eq:residual-loss-final}
\end{align}
The residual branch does not back-propagate through $\bar B$; background parameters receive their direct background supervision instead.

\subsection{Prediction Consistency and Joint Objective}

At inference the final estimate is
\begin{equation}
\hat V=\hat B+\hat R.
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:rs_pred_residual},
\begin{equation}
\hat V-V=\hat R-R_{\hat B}.
\label{eq:rs_composition_identity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} is an \emph{algebraic identity}. It states the error accounting induced by using the same background reference; it does not establish that the residual target is intrinsically easier to learn. Whether prediction-relative supervision and background context are useful is tested empirically by the controlled interventions in Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation}.

Joint optimization uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_R+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}+0.01\,\mathcal L_C ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_C$ is the role-aware condition objective. In the final configuration the two subset-Symile role terms have unit weight, anchor calibration has weight 0.5, and the reliability and two orthogonality regularizers each have weight 0.05; modality dropout is disabled. Supplementary Section~S1 gives the complete definitions.

The model is jointly optimized for 100 epochs with batch size 64 on four devices using DDP and bfloat16 mixed precision. AdamW is used without a learning-rate scheduler. Parameter-group learning rates are $10^{-6}$ for the encoder backbone, $3\times10^{-6}$ for encoder heads, the contrastive module, and condition adapters, $10^{-4}$ for the background branch, and $5\times10^{-5}$ for the residual branch. The joint run is initialized from previously trained condition, background, and residual stage checkpoints and then optimized under the joint objective. The complete evaluated model contains 10.6M trainable parameters.

\subsection{Inference and Evaluation Protocol}

At inference, only the observed constraints and their masks are supplied. BG-RFM predicts $\hat B$, samples one residual trajectory, and integrates the learned vector field for 50 Euler steps before composing $\hat V=\hat B+\hat R$. For the common comparison, stochastic BG-RFM predictions use base seed 2027; the per-batch seed is deterministically derived by hashing the ordered held-out record identities in that batch. The deterministic reference models require no sampling seed. No ensemble averaging over multiple stochastic trajectories is used.

The corpus contains 336,000 aligned records. A deterministic global 0.7/0.2/0.1 split with seed 42 yields 33,600 test records, and the evaluator traverses this membership with \texttt{shuffle=False}. The test manifest was verified to equal the reconstructed test split with zero train/validation, train/test, or validation/test overlap.

Velocity quantities in the common evaluator use the normalized OpenFWI target coordinate, corresponding to the physical interval 1500--4500~m/s mapped to $[-1,1]$. Normalized data loading clamps continuous inputs/targets to their declared profile range; horizon and well-mask channels remain discrete. The evaluator applies no additional post-hoc clipping to model predictions.

Metrics are computed per sample and then averaged over the 33,600 records. If $e_i=\hat V_i-V_i$ and $d=HW$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE}_i=\frac{\|e_i\|_1}{d},
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}_i=\sqrt{\frac{\|e_i\|_2^2}{d}}.
\end{equation}
The reported RMSE is therefore the mean of per-sample RMSE values rather than the square root of a pooled test-set MSE. SSIM is computed globally over each flattened $70\times70$ image using population variance/covariance, $C_{1,i}=(0.01L_i)^2$, $C_{2,i}=(0.03L_i)^2$, and
$L_i=\max(\max V_i-\min V_i,1)$; the resulting samplewise SSIM values are then averaged.

The matched-input references receive the same five observation channels: migrated image, RMS velocity, horizon, masked well value, and well mask. InversionNet, VelocityGAN, and the UPFWI-derived backbone use final checkpoints from fixed 100-epoch training schedules; Latent U-Net uses its minimum-validation-loss checkpoint; BG-RFM uses the final checkpoint of its fixed 100-epoch joint schedule. Test metrics are not used for checkpoint selection. Details of each adaptation and parameter-count convention are given in Supplementary Section~S5.
