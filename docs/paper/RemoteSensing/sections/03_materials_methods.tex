\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Task Definition and Synthetic Observation Contract}

This study considers velocity-model reconstruction when four processed or interpreted constraints are already available:
\[
X=\{M,H,V_{\mathrm{rms}},W\},
\]
where $M$ is a post-stack migrated image, $H$ is an interpreted-horizon map, $V_{\mathrm{rms}}$ is RMS velocity, and $W$ is a sparse well-velocity map with an associated mask. The target is the depth-domain velocity model
\[
V\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times70\times70}.
\]
The task is therefore not raw-shot end-to-end inversion. RMS velocity and the migrated image are represented on a $1000\times70$ time--lateral grid, while horizons, wells, and the target use the $70\times70$ depth grid.

The benchmark is constructed from FlatVel-A/B, CurveVel-A/B, FlatFault-A/B, and CurveFault-A/B in OpenFWI \citep{deng2022openfwi}. RMS velocity, the post-stack migrated image, and the horizon map are precomputed offline benchmark products that are read directly by the training pipeline. Their scientific construction is summarized below. Together with the sparse well constraints, these products define a \emph{controlled synthetic observation contract} that differs from a field setting in which RMS velocity, horizons, migration images, and wells carry heterogeneous estimation errors.

\paragraph{RMS velocity.}
For each lateral position, depth samples are mapped to two-way time through
\begin{equation}
t(z,x)=2\int_0^z \frac{1}{V(z',x)}\,\mathrm{d}z',
\end{equation}
and the RMS field is computed after interpolation to a uniform 1-ms time grid:
\begin{equation}
V_{\mathrm{rms}}^2(t,x)=\frac{1}{t}\int_0^t v^2(\tau,x)\,\mathrm{d}\tau.
\end{equation}
Because this quantity is derived directly from the known synthetic target, it can encode substantially more columnwise velocity information than an RMS product estimated from noisy, band-limited field observations. The benchmark therefore measures reconstruction under informative processed constraints, not performance under realistic RMS-estimation uncertainty.

\paragraph{Post-stack migrated image.}
OpenFWI shot records are processed by common-midpoint grouping, normal-moveout correction using the associated RMS field, stacking, and Kirchhoff post-stack time migration. The resulting image provides a dense structural observation on the time--lateral grid.

\paragraph{Horizons and wells.}
The benchmark horizon map is generated from vertical velocity-gradient magnitude using a fixed quantile threshold to form a binary structural map. Sparse wells retain zero to three selected lateral columns. For each selected column, the full depth trace is copied into the well-value tensor and the binary well mask is one along that column; values are zero outside selected columns. Validation and test wells are deterministic under well seed 1234.

\subsection{Role-Aware Multi-Source Conditioning}

The modalities differ in sampling domain, density, and information content, but BG-RFM does not impose a one-modality/one-role partition. Each modality is encoded into background-oriented and structure-oriented representations,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E_m(x_m)=\left(Z_m^{\mathrm{bg}},Z_m^{\mathrm{str}},r_m\right),
\end{equation}
where $r_m$ contains learned role-specific reliability scores. In the evaluated loader, the quality state does not use target error: a present non-well modality has $q_{i,m}=1$, while well quality is binary according to whether sampled well columns exist. Availability is $a_{i,m}=\mathbf{1}[\mathrm{mask}_{i,m}q_{i,m}>0]$. Learned reliability is produced by adaptive average pooling, a linear layer, and a sigmoid. Role weights are proportional to learned reliability times availability and are normalized over available modalities with denominator clamped at $10^{-6}$; if no modality is available, fused weights remain zero. The two role interfaces are
\begin{equation}
C_k=\mathcal G_k\left(\sum_{m\in\Omega}w_m^k Z_m^k\right),
\qquad k\in\{\mathrm{bg},\mathrm{str}\},
\end{equation}
with $\Omega$ the observed modality set. In the condition-learning objective, the corresponding roles are denoted numerical and structural; downstream, the numerical role forms the background-oriented interface $C_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and the structural role forms $C_{\mathrm{str}}$. RMS and post-stack migrated inputs are compressed/aligned to $70\times70$ spatial feature maps with 32 channels, while horizon and well streams produce 16-channel aligned maps; fusion occurs only after alignment to the depth-grid representation. RMS uses temporal pooling and bilinear alignment, while PSTM uses anisotropic downsampling and projection. Both $C_{\mathrm{bg}}$ and $C_{\mathrm{str}}$ are 32-channel $70\times70$ conditions. Training-only subset-Symile, anchor-calibration, separation, and reliability terms are defined below; their anchor tensors are not inference inputs. Inference requires only the observations, masks, and loader-provided availability/quality state, not any ground-truth-derived reliability score.

\subsection{Deterministic Background Prediction}\label{sec:methods-background}

The background branch is a full-resolution U-Net \citep{ronneberger2015unet} with 192 hidden channels:
\begin{equation}
\hat B=f_{\mathrm{bg}}(C_{\mathrm{bg}}).
\end{equation}
The supervision target uses the exact low-pass operator implemented throughout the study,
\begin{equation}
P_L(X)=\operatorname{AvgPool}_{5\times5}(X;\,\text{stride}=1,\text{padding}=2),
\end{equation}
with the framework's default inclusion of padded values in the average. The complementary operator is $P_H=I-P_L$. The active background objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}
=
\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_1
+0.5\,\|\hat B-P_L(V)\|_2^2.
\label{eq:bg-loss-final}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Prediction-Relative Flow Matching}

The residual target is defined relative to the background that is actually available at inference:
\begin{equation}
R_{\hat B}=V-\hat B.
\label{eq:rs_pred_residual}
\end{equation}
During residual optimization, the forward value of the prediction is preserved but detached from the residual gradient path,
\begin{equation}
\bar B=\operatorname{sg}[\hat B].
\end{equation}
The residual vector field receives the structural condition and a learned embedding of this detached background,
\begin{equation}
c=[C_{\mathrm{str}};\psi_{\mathrm{bg}}(\bar B)].
\end{equation}

BG-RFM uses the standard linear Flow-Matching path. For $t\sim\mathcal U(0,1)$ and $\xi\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$,
\begin{equation}
R_t=(1-t)\xi+t(V-\bar B),
\qquad
u_t=(V-\bar B)-\xi ,
\end{equation}
and the vector-field term is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
=
\mathbb E\left[
\|v_\theta(R_t,t,c)-u_t\|_2^2
\right].
\end{equation}
The evaluated residual network is a full-resolution FiLM-conditioned U-Net \citep{perez2018film} with 128 hidden channels and an identity codec on the $70\times70$ velocity/state grid. Training samples continuous time $t\in[0,1]$; inference integrates the learned vector field with 50 explicit Euler steps.

The prediction-relative formulation also has a simple full-field interpretation. Because the stop-gradient operator changes only the backward path, $\bar B$ and $\hat B$ have the same forward value. Defining
\begin{equation}
Y_t=\hat B+R_t
\end{equation}
therefore gives
\begin{equation}
Y_t=(1-t)(\hat B+\xi)+tV.
\label{eq:background-centered-path}
\end{equation}
In full-field coordinates, prediction-relative residual Flow Matching is therefore standard linear transport from a stochastic base centered on the predicted background toward $V$; the methodological distinction is the learned, observation-dependent reconstruction reference.

The residual loss also includes the composed reconstruction and the low-pass content of the actual prediction-relative residual:
\begin{align}
\widetilde R
&=R_t+(1-t)v_\theta(R_t,t,c),\\
\mathcal L_R
&=
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{FM}}
+\|\bar B+\widetilde R-V\|_1
+0.2\,\|P_L(\widetilde R)-P_L(V-\bar B)\|_1.
\label{eq:residual-loss-final}
\end{align}
The residual branch does not back-propagate through $\bar B$; background parameters receive their direct background supervision instead.

\subsection{Prediction Consistency and Joint Objective}

At inference the final estimate is
\begin{equation}
\hat V=\hat B+\hat R.
\end{equation}
Together with Eq.~\eqref{eq:rs_pred_residual},
\begin{equation}
\hat V-V=\hat R-R_{\hat B}.
\label{eq:rs_composition_identity}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} is an \emph{algebraic identity} that makes the error accounting induced by the shared background reference explicit.

Joint optimization uses
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L=\mathcal L_R+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{bg}}+0.01\,\mathcal L_C ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal L_C$ is the role-aware condition objective:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal L_C={}&
\mathcal L_{\mathrm{subsym}}^{\mathrm{str}}
+\mathcal L_{\mathrm{subsym}}^{\mathrm{num}}
+0.5\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{anchor}}\\
&+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{rel}}
+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{str,num}}^{\perp}
+0.05\,\mathcal L_{\mathrm{unique,role}}^{\perp}.
\end{split}
\label{eq:condition-objective}
\end{equation}
Here, one-level Haar decomposition of the target forms detached numerical (resized LL) and structural (L2 fusion of resized LH/HL/HH) training anchors, with replicate boundaries and nearest-neighbor resizing. Subset-Symile groups records by identical modality availability; same-record projected modality representations and their anchor form positive tuples, while within-group batch derangements provide up to batch-size-minus-one negatives. Groups smaller than two are skipped. Anchor calibration compares normalized modality projections and anchor projections using symmetric InfoNCE, with cosine-distance fallback for singleton groups. The reliability penalty measures squared deviation of learned role reliability from fixed modality-role priors. The two separation penalties are absolute cosine similarities between pooled structural/numerical maps and between pooled unique/role representations, respectively. Appendix~\ref{app:math} provides the reliability-prior values and gradient-path details.

Training proceeds in four stages: condition pretraining (100 epochs, batch 150, AdamW $10^{-4}$, validation retrieval top-5 selection), background pretraining (100 epochs, batch 100, AdamW $10^{-4}$, minimum validation loss), residual pretraining (100 epochs, batch 64, AdamW $5\times10^{-5}$, minimum validation loss, frozen codec), and joint optimization (100 epochs, batch 64 per process, grouped AdamW rates, minimum validation loss, frozen codec). Background pretraining initializes from the condition checkpoint; residual pretraining initializes from the background checkpoint; joint training warm-starts from all three. Final joint optimization uses four-device DDP with bfloat16 mixed precision and no learning-rate scheduler. Parameter-group learning rates are $10^{-6}$ for the encoder backbone, $3\times10^{-6}$ for encoder heads, the contrastive module, and condition adapters, $10^{-4}$ for the background branch, and $5\times10^{-5}$ for the residual branch. The evaluated inference model contains 10,630,216 trainable parameters (10.63M); the enclosing training wrapper contains 10,648,427 parameters because it also includes training-only state/modules.

\subsection{Inference and Evaluation Protocol}\label{sec:methods-evaluation}

At inference, only the observed constraints and their masks/availability state are supplied. BG-RFM predicts $\hat B$, samples one residual trajectory, and integrates the learned vector field for 50 Euler steps before composing $\hat V=\hat B+\hat R$. The evaluation batch size is 64 and the held-out loader uses \texttt{shuffle=False}. For the common comparison, stochastic BG-RFM predictions use base seed 2027; the per-batch seed is deterministically derived by hashing the ordered held-out record identities in that batch. This batch-keyed seed controls only the stochastic realization; dataset membership is fixed independently of batch grouping. The deterministic reference models require no sampling seed. No ensemble averaging over multiple stochastic trajectories is used.

The corpus contains 336,000 aligned records. A deterministic global 0.7/0.2/0.1 split with seed 42 yields 33,600 test records. All prerequisite and joint training stages invoke the same deterministic dataset-splitting implementation using this split contract; training and validation loaders access only their corresponding partitions, and test metrics are not used for checkpoint selection. The common evaluation manifest was verified against the reconstructed test split with zero train/validation, train/test, or validation/test overlap.

Velocity quantities in the common evaluator use the normalized OpenFWI target coordinate, corresponding to the physical interval 1500--4500~m/s mapped to $[-1,1]$. Normalized data loading clamps continuous inputs/targets to their declared profile range; horizon and well-mask channels remain discrete. The evaluator applies no additional post-hoc clipping to model predictions.

Metrics are computed per sample and then averaged over the 33,600 records. If $e_i=\hat V_i-V_i$ and $d=HW$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{MAE}_i=\frac{\|e_i\|_1}{d},
\qquad
\mathrm{RMSE}_i=\sqrt{\frac{\|e_i\|_2^2}{d}}.
\end{equation}
The reported RMSE is the mean of per-sample RMSE values:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{RMSE}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\sqrt{\frac{1}{HW}\|\hat V_i-V_i\|_2^2}.
\label{eq:eval-rmse}
\end{equation}
SSIM is computed globally for each sample as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{SSIM}_i=
\frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y+C_{1,i})(2\sigma_{xy}+C_{2,i})}
{(\mu_x^2+\mu_y^2+C_{1,i})(\sigma_x^2+\sigma_y^2+C_{2,i})}.
\label{eq:eval-ssim}
\end{equation}
SSIM is computed globally over each flattened $70\times70$ image using population variance/covariance, $C_{1,i}=(0.01L_i)^2$, $C_{2,i}=(0.03L_i)^2$, and
$L_i=\max(\max V_i-\min V_i,1)$; the resulting samplewise SSIM values are then averaged.

The matched-input references receive the same five observation channels: migrated image, RMS velocity, horizon, masked well value, and well mask. InversionNet, VelocityGAN, and the UPFWI-derived backbone use final checkpoints from fixed 100-epoch training schedules; Latent U-Net uses its minimum-validation-loss checkpoint; BG-RFM uses the validation-loss-selected checkpoint from the final joint stage. Test metrics are not used for checkpoint selection. Details of each adaptation and parameter-count convention are given in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.
