\section{Results}

\subsection{Overall Reconstruction Performance}

Table~\ref{tab:rs-main} reports the common-protocol comparison on the same 33,600 held-out records and with the same evaluator. It is a controlled comparison among multimodal estimators rather than a ranking of the original source-native methods.

\input{tables/main_quantitative}

BG-RFM obtains an RMSE of 0.02695 and an SSIM of 0.99165 with 10.6M trainable parameters. Its RMSE is numerically lower than that of the InversionNet adaptation (0.02695 versus 0.02730), and the corresponding SSIM values are 0.99165 and 0.99118; InversionNet has the lower MAE (0.01194 versus 0.01503). The differences between these two methods are reported descriptively and are not treated as evidence of statistical superiority. A similar metric trade-off occurs relative to the UPFWI-derived supervised multimodal backbone: BG-RFM has lower RMSE and higher SSIM, while the UPFWI-derived model has lower MAE. BG-RFM improves all three metrics relative to VelocityGAN. Latent U-Net obtains the lowest MAE and RMSE and the highest SSIM in this comparison.

BG-RFM has the smallest reported parameter count among the listed models. This supports a parameter-compact formulation, not a claim of lower inference latency: its residual sampler requires repeated vector-field evaluations.

\subsection{High-Margin Qualitative Examples}

Aggregate errors do not show where structural differences occur. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} therefore provides two deliberately performance-selected examples from complex OpenFWI subsets. They are \emph{not} intended as typical or representative cases. The selection rule is fixed and uses no visual inspection: within each subset, records are ranked by the formal SSIM margin
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{SSIM}}
=
\mathrm{SSIM}_{\mathrm{BG\text{-}RFM}}
-
\max_{m\in\mathcal B}\mathrm{SSIM}_m ,
\end{equation}
where the selection pool $\mathcal B$ contains the adapted InversionNet, VelocityGAN, UPFWI-derived supervised backbone, Auto-Linear, and Latent U-Net models. The main figure displays only the five methods used in Table~\ref{tab:rs-main}; Auto-Linear participated in the original selection pool but is not shown in the main comparison.

\input{figures/figure3_qualitative}

The examples illustrate cases in which the background-guided formulation preserves coherent large-scale velocity organization together with structural corrections. Because the cases were selected by a performance margin, they cannot be used to estimate typical visual performance. Supplementary Section~S6 therefore also reports the broader per-subset high-margin panel and explicit failure cases.

\subsection{Controlled Design Analysis}

Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation} reports a controlled design study in which the observation contract, training budget, and held-out protocol are held fixed while reconstruction organization is changed. The table makes the prediction target, background/residual reference, conditioning, and composition explicit for each intervention.

\input{tables/design_ablation}

Within this study, the single-condition full-field Flow-Matching model has MAE/RMSE/SSIM of 0.0225/0.0340/0.9865, while the role-aware full-field variant reaches 0.0205/0.0320/0.9880. In the background-guided formulation, replacing reliability-aware fusion with uniform fusion yields 0.0163/0.0290/0.9898. More directly related to prediction consistency, changing residual supervision from $V-\hat B$ to $V-P_L(V)$ while retaining predicted-background context and composition increases MAE from 0.0150 to 0.0190 and RMSE from 0.0270 to 0.0340. Removing the predicted-background context while retaining prediction-relative supervision increases RMSE to 0.0410 and lowers SSIM to 0.9820. These interventions provide the empirical evidence for the usefulness of the shared predicted-background reference; the algebraic identity in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} alone does not establish a learnability advantage.

\subsection{Diagnostic Analysis}

Two diagnostics further characterize the formulation. First, the low-pass background and its structural complement have different participation-ratio effective ranks across the eight training subsets. The median effective ranks are 4.95 for the background and 54.03 for the structural complement, with the structural component higher in every subset. This is a descriptive property of the chosen decomposition, not a proof that the conditional entropy of the residual is lower.

Second, the learned condition interfaces and residual transport can be inspected after training. The condition analysis gives matched--deranged anchor-similarity margins of 0.46 for the background role and 0.35 for the structural role. For the straight-path transport diagnostic, every one of the 33,600 held-out records satisfies $q_T<1$, with mean $q_T=0.772$ and bootstrap 95\% confidence interval $[0.771,0.773]$. In the stated normalized coordinate system this means that the background-centered path has lower samplewise straight-path target energy than the zero-centered full-field path. It is a diagnostic observation, not a causal explanation of reconstruction accuracy.
