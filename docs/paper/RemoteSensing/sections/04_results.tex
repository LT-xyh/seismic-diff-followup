\section{Results}

\subsection{Overall Reconstruction Performance}

Table~\ref{tab:rs-main} compares multimodal estimators on the same 33,600 held-out records using the same evaluator. The comparison concerns adaptations to a common processed-constraint task, rather than source-native protocols.

\input{tables/main_quantitative}

The Latent U-Net adaptation achieves the strongest aggregate reconstruction accuracy, with MAE 0.00706, RMSE 0.01309, and SSIM 0.99403. BG-RFM attains MAE 0.01503, RMSE 0.02695, and SSIM 0.99165 with 10.63M trainable inference parameters, compared with 35.12M for the Latent U-Net adaptation. These results distinguish the contributions: Latent U-Net provides the lowest aggregate reconstruction error, while BG-RFM demonstrates prediction-referenced generative correction with explicitly allocated deterministic-background and residual-transport roles in a smaller parameter footprint. BG-RFM also obtains lower RMSE and higher SSIM than the InversionNet and UPFWI-derived adaptations, with higher MAE, and improves all three reported metrics relative to VelocityGAN. Because BG-RFM integrates its residual vector field over 50 Euler steps, parameter count alone does not establish a latency advantage.

\subsection{High-Margin Qualitative Examples}\label{sec:results-qualitative}

Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} illustrates structural reconstructions in two complex OpenFWI cases selected by a predefined, performance-dependent criterion rather than visual inspection. Within each subset, the selection maximizes the BG-RFM SSIM margin over the strongest competing result:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{SSIM}}
=
\mathrm{SSIM}_{\mathrm{BG\text{-}RFM}}
-
\max_{m\in\mathcal B}\mathrm{SSIM}_m ,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal B$ includes the adapted InversionNet, VelocityGAN, UPFWI-derived supervised backbone, Auto-Linear, and Latent U-Net models. These are intentionally favorable high-margin examples, not estimates of typical visual performance. The figure displays the five methods in Table~\ref{tab:rs-main}; Auto-Linear belongs to the selection pool but is not shown.

\subsection{Supporting Comparison with Alternative Flow-Matching Formulations}

Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation} compares three complete reconstruction formulations. Concat-FM directly transports the full velocity field from concatenated heterogeneous conditions; role-aware full-field FM uses the learned background- and structure-oriented interfaces but still transports the complete velocity field. BG-RFM instead predicts a deterministic background and transports the correction relative to that same prediction, which also provides residual context and enters final composition.

\input{tables/design_ablation}

In the retained study, BG-RFM gives MAE/RMSE/SSIM of 0.0150/0.0270/0.9913, compared with 0.0225/0.0340/0.9865 for Concat-FM and 0.0205/0.0320/0.9880 for role-aware full-field FM. These descriptive results support the complete background-referenced reconstruction formulation without assigning the observed differences to any isolated component.

\subsection{Diagnostic Analysis}

The decomposition and learned conditions offer complementary ways to characterize the model. Across eight training subsets, the median participation-ratio effective ranks are 4.95 for the chosen low-pass background and 54.03 for its structural complement, with higher structural effective rank in every subset. The matched--deranged anchor-similarity margins are 0.46 for the background role and 0.35 for the structural role.

The Gaussian-base-averaged straight-path target-energy ratio is below one for all 33,600 held-out records, with mean $q_T=0.772$ and bootstrap 95\% confidence interval $[0.771,0.773]$. In the evaluated normalized coordinates, this characterizes a lower straight-path target energy for the background-centered reference than for the zero-centered full-field reference. These diagnostics describe the selected decomposition, condition alignment, and transport target; they are not estimates of isolated causal effects.
