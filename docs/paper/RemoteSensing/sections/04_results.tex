\section{Results}

\subsection{Overall Reconstruction Performance}

Table~\ref{tab:rs-main} reports the common-protocol comparison on the same 33,600 held-out records and with the same evaluator. It is a controlled comparison among multimodal estimators rather than a ranking of the original source-native methods.

\input{tables/main_quantitative}

BG-RFM obtains an RMSE of 0.02695 and an SSIM of 0.99165 with 10.63M trainable parameters. Relative to the InversionNet adaptation, BG-RFM has lower RMSE (0.02695 versus 0.02730) and higher SSIM (0.99165 versus 0.99118), while InversionNet has lower MAE (0.01194 versus 0.01503). A similar trade-off occurs relative to the UPFWI-derived supervised multimodal backbone: BG-RFM has lower RMSE and higher SSIM, whereas the UPFWI-derived model has lower MAE. BG-RFM improves all three reported reconstruction metrics relative to VelocityGAN. These results position BG-RFM as a compact structured alternative within the common multimodal reconstruction setting rather than as a single-metric benchmark claim.

BG-RFM uses 10.63M trainable inference parameters, fewer than the exact counts reported for the InversionNet, VelocityGAN training model, and Latent U-Net rows. Because an exact evaluated recount is unavailable for the UPFWI-derived backbone, no parameter comparison is made for that row. Parameter compactness is not a claim of lower inference latency: BG-RFM requires repeated vector-field evaluations.

\subsection{High-Margin Qualitative Examples}

Aggregate errors do not show where structural differences occur. Figure~\ref{fig:qualitative} therefore provides two deliberately performance-selected examples from complex OpenFWI subsets. They are \emph{not} intended as typical or representative cases. The selection rule is fixed and uses no visual inspection: within each subset, records are ranked by the formal SSIM margin
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{\mathrm{SSIM}}
=
\mathrm{SSIM}_{\mathrm{BG\text{-}RFM}}
-
\max_{m\in\mathcal B}\mathrm{SSIM}_m ,
\end{equation}
where the selection pool $\mathcal B$ contains the adapted InversionNet, VelocityGAN, UPFWI-derived supervised backbone, Auto-Linear, and Latent U-Net models. The main figure displays only the five methods used in Table~\ref{tab:rs-main}; Auto-Linear participated in the original selection pool but is not shown in the main comparison.

\input{figures/figure3_qualitative}

The examples illustrate cases in which the background-guided formulation preserves coherent large-scale velocity organization together with structural corrections. Because the cases were selected by a performance margin, they cannot be used to estimate typical visual performance. Appendix~\ref{app:additional} therefore also reports the broader per-subset high-margin panel and explicit failure cases.

\subsection{Supporting Comparison with Alternative Flow-Matching Formulations}

Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation} is a supporting formulation comparison distinct from the common matched-input evaluation of Table~\ref{tab:rs-main}. The retained runs share the same dataset family, split contract, normalization, target, metric definitions, optimizer family, and validation-based model selection; exact solver-step equality is not asserted across every formulation. Concat-FM mixes the heterogeneous condition features and applies Flow Matching directly to the full velocity field, with no explicit background/residual decomposition. Role-aware full-field FM retains the learned background-oriented and structure-oriented condition interfaces but still performs direct full-field Flow Matching. BG-RFM adds a separately supervised deterministic background prediction and applies Flow Matching to the correction defined relative to that prediction, using the same predicted background as residual context and in final composition.

\input{tables/design_ablation}

In this supporting formulation study, Concat-FM gives MAE/RMSE/SSIM of 0.0225/0.0340/0.9865, role-aware full-field FM gives 0.0205/0.0320/0.9880, and BG-RFM gives 0.0150/0.0270/0.9913. The complete background-guided residual formulation therefore has lower reconstruction errors and higher SSIM than the concatenated and role-aware full-field Flow-Matching alternatives in this study. Because the comparison changes the overall reconstruction formulation, it supports the complete organization at formulation level rather than isolating the contribution of any single component.

\subsection{Diagnostic Analysis}

Two diagnostics further characterize the formulation. First, the low-pass background and its structural complement have different participation-ratio effective ranks across the eight training subsets. The median effective ranks are 4.95 for the background and 54.03 for the structural complement, with the structural component higher in every subset. This is a descriptive property of the chosen decomposition, not a proof that the conditional entropy of the residual is lower.

Second, the learned condition interfaces and residual transport can be inspected after training. The condition analysis gives matched--deranged anchor-similarity margins of 0.46 for the background role and 0.35 for the structural role. For the Gaussian-base-averaged straight-path target-energy diagnostic, every one of the 33,600 held-out records satisfies $q_T<1$, with mean $q_T=0.772$ and bootstrap 95\% confidence interval $[0.771,0.773]$. In the stated normalized coordinate system this means that the background-centered path has lower samplewise Gaussian-base-averaged straight-path target energy than the zero-centered full-field path. It is a diagnostic observation, not a causal explanation of reconstruction accuracy.
