\section{Discussion}

BG-RFM makes reconstruction responsibility explicit. All observed modalities can contribute to both learned condition roles, while the downstream estimators have distinct assignments: a deterministic branch estimates the background and a conditional Flow-Matching branch models the correction relative to that estimate. Prediction consistency couples these assignments through one inference-available reference, because the same predicted background defines residual supervision, conditions the residual vector field, and enters final composition. In full-field coordinates, Eq.~\eqref{eq:background-centered-path} expresses the resulting process as a straight transport from a background-centered stochastic base toward the target velocity model.

The supporting formulation comparison in Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation} is consistent with this reconstruction organization: the complete BG-RFM formulation yields lower reconstruction errors and higher SSIM than the two simpler full-field Flow-Matching alternatives in the retained study. The runs share the dataset family, split contract, normalization, target, metric definitions, optimizer family, and validation-based model selection. Because exact solver-step equality is not asserted across every retained formulation, the comparison is interpreted at formulation level rather than as an isolated attribution of prediction consistency, background context, or any single fusion mechanism.

The common matched-input evaluation provides the complementary system-level view. BG-RFM reaches RMSE 0.02695 and SSIM 0.99165 with 10.63M trainable inference parameters. Relative to the InversionNet and UPFWI-derived adaptations, it improves RMSE and SSIM while exhibiting a different MAE trade-off, and it improves all three reported reconstruction metrics relative to VelocityGAN. Its parameter footprint is also smaller than the exact counts reported for the InversionNet, VelocityGAN training model, and Latent U-Net rows. Parameter compactness should be interpreted separately from runtime: BG-RFM integrates the residual vector field for 50 Euler steps, whereas deterministic references require a single forward prediction.

The observation-removal analysis further shows that the trained model depends unevenly on the available constraints, with particularly strong sensitivity to RMS velocity and horizons. This behavior is consistent with the information content of the controlled synthetic observation contract: the RMS field is generated directly from the reference velocity model and can therefore carry stronger columnwise velocity information than a field-derived RMS product. The present results consequently characterize two-dimensional OpenFWI-derived reconstruction from informative processed/interpreted constraints. Field transfer, three-dimensional deployment, and downstream FWI or migration effects require separate validation protocols and remain outside the scope of this study.
