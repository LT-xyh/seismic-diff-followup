\section{Discussion}

The reconstruction problem considered here is shaped by an unusual combination of information sources. RMS velocity provides a broad kinematic description, migrated imagery and horizons emphasize structure, and sparse wells constrain velocity locally in depth. Encoding these observations jointly is necessary, but their fusion does not prescribe how a velocity field should be recovered from the resulting representation. BG-RFM addresses this second decision explicitly. Its background-oriented condition drives deterministic estimation of a smooth component, whereas its structure-oriented condition guides a Flow-Matching model that transports the remaining correction. The two tasks are coupled through the predicted background itself. By retaining this quantity as a reference rather than absorbing it into a general latent feature, the method gives an operational meaning to the distinction between background estimation and residual reconstruction.

The shared reference is important because the background used to construct a correction target during training must also be available when the model is deployed on unseen observations. Using the actual background prediction for residual supervision avoids substituting an inaccessible oracle background during inference. The same prediction enters the residual vector field as context and is then used in additive composition, so the residual generator learns to correct the estimate that will be used in the final result. Equation~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} expresses the resulting error accounting exactly. Moreover, Eq.~\eqref{eq:background-centered-path} shows that the residual path can be understood in full-field coordinates as linear transport from a background-centered stochastic base toward the target velocity. This interpretation situates BG-RFM within standard Flow Matching while clarifying what the background predictor changes: not the transport mathematics, but the observation-dependent origin and context of the generated correction.

The numerical evidence illustrates both the scope and the trade-offs of that choice. Under the common multimodal protocol, BG-RFM achieves high structural similarity with substantially fewer trainable inference parameters than the listed InversionNet and Latent U-Net adaptations. The Latent U-Net nevertheless attains the best aggregate MAE, RMSE, and SSIM and performs direct regression with a single forward prediction. The empirical case for BG-RFM is therefore not universal reconstruction dominance or faster inference. It is the availability of a compact generative alternative whose reconstruction responsibilities are explicit and whose correction is referenced to a supervised background estimate. The separate Flow-Matching formulation study is consistent with this organization, but because it changes more than one design choice and does not establish identical solver settings for every retained run, it cannot identify a unique cause of the observed metric differences.

The descriptive analyses help interpret that architecture without substituting for controlled attribution. The effective-rank measurements characterize a low-pass component with more concentrated variation than its structural complement under the chosen filter. Anchor-alignment diagnostics show how the learned condition representations relate to their training-time numerical and structural references. Likewise, the straight-path energy ratio quantifies the shift associated with moving the stochastic base from zero toward the predicted background. The ratio being below one throughout the evaluated held-out set characterizes the selected transport targets; it is not a theorem that residual transport must optimize more easily or give higher reconstruction accuracy. Considered together with the formulation comparison, the diagnostics motivate why an observation-dependent reference is an interpretable modeling choice, while leaving its isolated optimization benefits an empirical question.

The declared observation contract is essential to interpreting the geophysical scope. Unlike an RMS product estimated from semblance analysis or uncertain velocity picking, the synthetic RMS field used here is integrated directly from the known reference velocity model. The horizon masks are likewise derived from reference velocity gradients, and the migrated images are generated through a controlled processing sequence. These products make the benchmark useful for assessing reconstruction from informative and mutually related constraints, but they do not reproduce all the estimation errors present in field interpretation. The marked deterioration when RMS velocity or horizons are removed (Appendix~\ref{app:additional}) demonstrates the trained model's dependence on those observations within the full-input protocol; it should not be interpreted as an isolated causal measure of each modality's contribution. Accordingly, the present evidence establishes behavior on two-dimensional OpenFWI-derived velocity fields with precomputed geophysical constraints, rather than direct field transfer, three-dimensional applicability, or downstream improvements in inversion and migration.

Within these boundaries, the broader methodological point is that multimodal conditioning and generative reconstruction need not be organized as a single full-field prediction operation. A deterministic estimate can provide a data-dependent reference for a subsequent generative process, and the same reference can be maintained across supervision, conditional transport, and composition. For initial velocity model building, that organization reflects the distinction between regional kinematic recovery and the corrections required to reconstruct interfaces and faults. Its value lies in making those reconstruction responsibilities explicit while retaining the ability to use heterogeneous evidence throughout the model.
