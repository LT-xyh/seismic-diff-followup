\section{Discussion}

BG-RFM makes reconstruction responsibility explicit. All observed modalities can contribute to both learned condition roles, but the downstream tasks differ: a deterministic branch estimates the background and a conditional Flow-Matching branch models the correction relative to that estimate. The method therefore changes the organization of a multimodal reconstruction problem rather than introducing a new Flow-Matching equation.

The full-field interpretation in Eq.~\eqref{eq:background-centered-path} makes this distinction precise. Adding $\hat B$ to the residual path converts it to a standard straight path from the background-centered stochastic base $\hat B+\xi$ toward $V$. Prediction consistency is a defining property of the proposed formulation: the same inference-available $\hat B$ defines residual supervision, conditions the vector field, and enters final composition. Equation~\eqref{eq:rs_composition_identity} is only an algebraic error identity and is not evidence that prediction consistency alone improves accuracy. Table~\ref{tab:design-ablation} instead supports the complete background-guided residual formulation at the formulation level, showing lower reconstruction errors than the retained concatenated and role-aware full-field Flow-Matching alternatives. The study does not isolate the individual performance contribution of prediction consistency or predicted-background conditioning.

The common-protocol comparison also defines the scope of the accuracy claim. Latent U-Net obtains the strongest MAE, RMSE, and SSIM among the listed models, while InversionNet and the UPFWI-derived supervised multimodal backbone obtain lower MAE than BG-RFM. BG-RFM is therefore not presented as the overall accuracy winner. Its contribution is the explicit deterministic/generative responsibility allocation and the reuse of the predicted background within a comparatively compact parameterization.

The 10.6M-parameter footprint is an architectural property rather than a proxy for computational speed. Residual inference uses 50 Euler steps and therefore requires repeated vector-field evaluations, whereas deterministic references require a single forward prediction. No faster-inference claim is made.

The observation-removal analysis in Supplementary Section~S3 should also be interpreted narrowly. The degradation differs substantially when individual observation types are removed, demonstrating different dependence on the available constraints. It does not isolate a causal contribution for each modality, because the model was trained under the full multimodal observation contract and the inputs are statistically coupled.

The RMS field deserves particular caution. In this benchmark it is generated from the reference velocity model through a known depth-to-time transform and RMS integration. It is therefore unusually informative and can encode substantially more columnwise velocity information than an RMS field estimated from field data. The reported results characterize a controlled synthetic reconstruction problem with informative processed/interpreted constraints. They should not be read as evidence that the same accuracy will hold when RMS picking, migration, horizon interpretation, and wells are uncertain.

The current study is also limited to two-dimensional OpenFWI-derived models. It does not establish field transfer, three-dimensional deployment, or downstream FWI/migration gains. Those questions require different observation-error and validation protocols and are outside the bounded claims of the present work.
