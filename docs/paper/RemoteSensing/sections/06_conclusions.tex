\section{Conclusions}

Initial velocity reconstruction from heterogeneous geophysical constraints is not only a question of how to fuse observations but also of how to assign the information they contain to different parts of a reconstruction. BG-RFM approaches this problem by estimating a smooth background deterministically and transporting the correction relative to that inference-available estimate. The defining feature is the consistent use of the same predicted background to construct residual supervision, condition the Flow-Matching vector field, and compose the final velocity model. In this way, background recovery and generative correction remain distinct computational responsibilities while being linked by a shared physical-space reference.

The eight-subset OpenFWI-derived evaluation demonstrates this formulation within a controlled processed-constraint setting. Across 33,600 common held-out records, BG-RFM attains RMSE 0.02695 and SSIM 0.99165 with 10.63M trainable inference parameters. The matched-input comparison establishes its place among adapted deterministic and generative estimators, while the formulation study and representation/transport diagnostics provide complementary, descriptive evidence for the proposed reconstruction organization. The contribution is not a new Flow-Matching equation or universal accuracy superiority, but a clearly specified way to connect multimodal conditioning with deterministic background estimation and prediction-relative generative modeling.

The central methodological lesson is therefore one of reconstruction design: \emph{predict the background; transport the prediction-relative correction}. For initial seismic velocity model building, this provides an interpretable alternative to assigning an entire velocity field to a single undifferentiated predictor.
