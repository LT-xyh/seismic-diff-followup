# Publication Principles

These rules govern the Remote Sensing revision.

## Core principle: the paper is a scientific launch

The manuscript is not a project report, experiment log, rebuttal diary, or self-audit document. It should communicate the strongest publishable scientific value with a coherent, evidence-backed story.

## Writing rules

1. **Organize around strengths.** Prioritize results that are leading, distinctive, or necessary to establish the method's value.
2. **Do not write chronologically.** Do not narrate trial-and-error history. Present only the final scientific logic: important problem -> specific gap -> proposed solution -> supporting evidence.
3. **Do not volunteer broad negative judgments.** Avoid language that converts a local limitation into a verdict on the entire method.
4. **Do not create unnecessary competitions.** Do not define the paper around dimensions that are unrelated to the retained core claim.
5. **When a dimension is not central, narrow the claim rather than manufacture a win.** Required standard metrics remain reportable; the narrative emphasis follows the scientific objective.
6. **State the advantages explicitly.** Explain where the method helps, why the result matters, and what design element supports the gain.
7. **Control the comparison scope.** Claims and experiments must match. A paper does not need to dominate every metric to make a strong contribution.
8. **Experiments are arguments, not inventory.** Every main-paper experiment must have a clear argumentative role.
9. **Allow story reconstruction.** If the strongest evidence supports a different framing than the original AAAI narrative, change the story.
10. **Do not create unnecessary reviewer attack surfaces.** Avoid claims broader than the evidence, unnecessary universal statements, or ambiguous terminology.
11. **Abstract and introduction are the launch.** Quickly establish the problem, gap, distinct solution, strongest evidence, and significance.
12. **Conclusion reinforces memory.** Restate what was solved, what was introduced, and what was demonstrated. Do not introduce new self-criticism at the end.

## Default decision rule for weak or distracting material

Apply in this order:

1. remove material unrelated to the core claim;
2. narrow the claim;
3. select a more relevant evaluation dimension;
4. explain a genuine task/operating tradeoff when supported by evidence;
5. restructure figures/tables around the strongest evidence;
6. redefine the paper story if needed;
7. include unavoidable caveats only where they materially affect interpretation.

## Integrity constraint

Publication strategy never permits:
- hiding required experimental facts;
- altering results;
- omitting a comparison needed to substantiate a claim;
- presenting cross-protocol numbers as matched comparisons;
- calling an unreplicated result replicated;
- converting a failed experiment into a positive result through wording.

The principle is **selective scientific storytelling, not selective truth**.
